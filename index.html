02 ORIGINAL BRAND AUDIT
blissclub

BlissClub — a strategic teardown

BlissClub has fixed a real gap in the Indian market of activewear for all body types. They have also brought a wide category of products including swimwear, athleisure and menswear. They have collaborated with influencers like Sakshi Sidhwani — a milestone reached. This is a self-initiated analysis of what more they could do and what they will have to stop in order to have a great impression going forward.

◎ Positioning

BlissClub is built for real bodies — a promise it communicates effectively through its choice of influencer partnerships. This consumer-centric approach sets it apart from competitors in the Indian activewear market.

👥 Target Audience

Women aged 24-38 who lead active, everyday lifestyles. With the recently launched menswear line, the audience now extends to men in the same age bracket — keeping the demographic consistent while broadening reach.

✨ Influencer Strategy

BlissClub has grown through mid-tier influencer collaborations, but there is significant potential in activations that let customers experience the product first-hand — such as influencer-led events and in-store touchpoints. Targeting micro-influencers with strong content and engaged but growing audiences would build authentic word-of-mouth, positioning the brand as one that nurtures rising talent. Couple influencers showcasing day-in-the-life content could further integrate the product into natural, relatable contexts.

✍️ The Gap

Recent AI-generated imagery quietly contradicts a promise built on real women, real bodies and an honest, inclusive community. For a brand whose equity is authenticity and belonging, synthetic visuals risk eroding the exact trust it sells — and muddy the story just as it enters menswear.

My Perspective

I'd strengthen BlissClub's authenticity by doubling down on nano-influencer UGC — real customers in everyday workouts — to reinforce the "real women, real movement" promise while lowering Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC). Community initiatives like run clubs and in-store experiences would turn buyers into a movement, and the menswear line should be introduced as a natural extension of that same inclusive community.

Beyond fitness creators, I'd also tap lifestyle and couple influencers — travel creators, young couples, new parents — who naturally incorporate athleisure into daily life. This positions BlissClub as versatile everyday wear, not just activewear, broadening appeal organically.

I'd reserve AI as a behind-the-scenes tool for ideation and production, keeping customer-facing campaigns rooted in real people. For a brand like BlissClub, authenticity is the strongest differentiator — every touchpoint should reflect that.
